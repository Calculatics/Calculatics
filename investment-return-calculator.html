How the Investment Return Calculator works

This calculator measures the gain or loss on an investment after accounting for additional contributions and withdrawals.

It compares the value remaining in your investment, plus withdrawals received, against the total capital contributed.

Investment return formula

Investment gain or loss = Ending value + Withdrawals − Starting value − Additional contributions.

Total investment return (%) = (Investment gain or loss ÷ Total capital contributed) × 100.

Total capital contributed = Starting value + Additional contributions.

Investment return example

Suppose your investment starts at $10,000. You contribute another $2,000 and withdraw $500 during the investment period.

If your remaining investment is worth $15,000, your investment gain is $3,500.

Your total capital contributed is $12,000, giving a simple investment return of approximately 29.17%.

What is annualized return?

Annualized return expresses an investment's overall return as an equivalent compounded annual rate.

This calculator annualizes the simple total return percentage over the investment period. It is an approximation when contributions or withdrawals occur during the period.

Important: contributions and withdrawals

The dates of contributions and withdrawals affect an investment's true annualized performance.

This calculator uses aggregate cash-flow amounts rather than individual transaction dates. Its total return is a simple capital-based return, not a time-weighted investment return.

For investments with cash flows occurring on different dates, an XIRR calculation or time-weighted return calculation may provide a more meaningful performance measure.

Important considerations

Enter the ending value after withdrawals have been taken out. Do not include those withdrawals again in the ending value.

Investment fees and taxes are not separately calculated. If they have already reduced the ending investment value, their effect is reflected in the result.

This calculator is intended for estimating investment performance and should not replace detailed portfolio accounting or professional financial advice.
